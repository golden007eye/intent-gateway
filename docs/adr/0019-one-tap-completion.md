# ADR-0019: One-tap completion: step machine, compare-and-append, restarts

Status: accepted · 2026-10-17 · Relates to ADR-0015 (cap release on local evidence), ADR-0016 (completer key); amends ADR-0017 (checkout ids, checkout ref)

## Context

A verified `shop_agent` approval (contract 1.6.0, Appendix G) is completed by the gateway with the developer's linked Shop wallet (ADR-0017). Spending real money from an agent's tool call has to hold across crashes, restarts and two gateway processes on one data dir:

- never two `complete_checkout` sends for one approval, and never one the chain does not show was owned and logged first;
- a send that may have left is never repeated, whether or not Shop honours idempotency keys;
- spend-cap room comes back only on evidence the gateway holds itself (ADR-0015);
- Intent Cloud has received and accepted the redemption report before the wallet is charged (Appendix G completer precheck).

Shop's checkout id can carry a credential (`?key=`), so the chain names a checkout only by its hash, and the adapter held the raw id in memory only. A restarted gateway that may have completed a checkout must still be able to read it, or every crash after a send would end `indeterminate` for good.

## Decision

- **Driven from `get_approval_status`, never in the background.** `src/gateway/complete.ts` runs inside the status call, after the approval is chained, on both status paths (a new approval, and one already chained, which is the path every resume after a restart takes), until the first wait state or 20 s. Every request to Intent Cloud the gateway signs here uses the approval's completer key (ADR-0016).
- **Steps, each chained before its effect.** Preconditions (policy allows one_tap, a wallet, before `expires_at`); the effective per-order cap `min(policy, wallet budget)`; the quote (`create_checkout`) and binding-v1 checks 1, 3, 4 and 5 on it; the `completion` entry; the `completing` report; the `complete_attempt` entry; the completer precheck on a fresh `get_checkout` (same process, no cached read: check 2 against the completion's hash, checks 1, 3, 4, 5, expiry, and Appendix E again, step 7 included); `complete_checkout`; the local `outcome`; the report of it. A refusal before Intent Cloud accepted `completing` is a local outcome only; after it, it is reported as `failed` / `gateway_precheck`.
- **Compare-and-append in the store.** `Store.appendUnless(kind, body, conflict)` evaluates `conflict` over the chain while holding the lease, after reading every entry other writers appended and checking the tail, and before the write. A conflict throws `StoreConflictError`, writes nothing, and does not fence the handle: a lost race is an answer. One `completion` and one `complete_attempt` per approval are appended this way.
- **The send itself is compare-and-appended.** The disclosure core takes an `unless` condition for a send: the `complete_checkout` disclosure is appended, and the request sent, only while the attempt's owner token (random, memory only) is this process's, the attempt is younger than the attempt window, no `complete_checkout` that may be this purchase's is logged and no outcome is chained. Otherwise nothing is logged or sent (`DisclosureConflictError`). The core numbers one send at a time, from numbering to its append, so a lost condition gives its number back and never stops the core. `createDisclosedWallet` refuses a `complete_checkout` without its `purchase` or its condition before anything is logged, so the adapter cannot send one unconditionally.
- **One rule for "may have been sent".** `mayHaveSent(chain, H)` (`src/outcome`) is true for a `complete_checkout` disclosure logged after H's `approval` whose local `purchase` field is H, or is missing or not a valid approval handle (ambiguous, so it fails closed). Another purchase's send, named as such, does not count. The step machine, `chargedNothing` and `intent verify` use this one function.
- **The attempt window.** Within `ATTEMPT_WINDOW_MS` (60 s: the run's 20 s budget plus the wallet's 30 s timeout, rounded up) of a `complete_attempt`, a live owner may still be between claiming it and sending. Another process then treats an attempt without a send as a wait (`completing`), never as `not_sent`; after the window the owner itself refuses to send. The two verdicts can never meet.
- **Atomic cap reservation.** `approval_pending`, the entry that reserves cap room, is compare-and-appended against the policy's spend check over the chain, and the `completion` entry against the same check with this purchase counted once: two carts in two processes cannot both fit under one cap, and a cap that no longer fits at redemption ends the purchase `failed` (`cap`) before anything is sent to the wallet.
- **Reconcile, never re-send.** An owner token dies with its process, so after a restart an attempt is only reconciled. Past the attempt window, an attempt for which `mayHaveSent` is false was provably never sent (log before send): `failed` (`gateway_precheck`, `not_sent`), compare-and-appended against such a disclosure, so it and a send exclude each other. When `mayHaveSent` is true: `get_checkout` only. Open, not found, or an error is `indeterminate`, reported once, with the reservation held past `expires_at`.
- **Reports from the chain.** The `completing` report's body is fixed by the `completion` entry (`report_id`, checkout ref hash, `observed_at`), and every later report's by its first logged disclosure, so a retry after a restart resends the byte-identical body (R2). Report ids are `or_` + 22 base64url characters from the CSPRNG; every later report carries `completing_report_id`. Only a 201 with a verified receipt that was not discarded is an acceptance; only 400, 404, 409 and 410 refuse a report for good, while 401, 429 and 5xx leave it to retry.
- **`intent verify` audits the double-charge invariant.** Every `complete_checkout` disclosure names its purchase, comes after that purchase's `complete_attempt`, and is the only one for it; anything else fails verify.
- **Raw checkout ids in an encrypted checkout vault, keyed by checkout ref.** Chosen over `search_orders`: an order search cannot tell an open checkout from a lost one, carries no checkout id for binding check 2, and returns the buyer's whole order history. The Shop adapter keeps each raw id AES-256-GCM sealed under the wallet key (ADR-0013's vault, `src/wallet`), one 0600 file per checkout in `<data>/wallet/shop-checkouts/<hex of the ref>.enc`, with the AAD naming the rail and the ref, so a file copied under another ref's name does not open. Keyed by the checkout ref rather than the approval handle so the adapter owns it end to end and the gateway never sees a raw id. A file is deleted once the purchase has a terminal outcome, and any file older than 73 h (the completer read window after a 15-minute snapshot, plus a day) when the vault is opened. The checkout ref is the contract's checkout ref hash of the part before `?` (amendment L1), so reads with a different `?key=` name the same checkout.
- **Where Shop's store comes from is configurable and fails closed.** Shop names a store by its myshopify domain. Until the alias rule is decided (S1, D7), the default resolves only a storefront origin that is itself a myshopify host. A cart for any other merchant does not offer `shop_agent` at all, so it is handed off from the start instead of failing after the buyer's tap. Demo mode and tests map the fake storefront to the fake store. S1 note: Shop may report the custom storefront domain even for a myshopify store, so the alias rule must accept both directions before check 1 is frozen for `shop_agent`.
- **The checkout id is an opaque id to the identity scan.** Shop issues the checkout id that get_checkout and complete_checkout send back, and its random token or `?key=` can read as a card number by chance (13 to 19 digits that pass Luhn). The identity scan then refused the purchase's own fresh read, and the purchase waited out the attempt window and ended `failed` (`not_sent`): seen as a rare flaky test. A wallet destination may now declare opaque ids, exempt from the scan at exactly their path while the whole value matches a strict pattern; Shop's is a Checkout gid with an alphanumeric token and an optional alphanumeric key, so no text, `@` or space fits. The id stays digested in the log.
- **Tidy-up.** A quote that goes no further (refused at step 3, or lost in the step-4 race) has its checkout id forgotten at once. A terminal local outcome with no report ever accepted (a refusal before redemption) deletes the completer key: nothing is left to report.

## Consequences

- Two gateway processes, a crash at any step and a restart each end in one send at most, an `ordered`, `mismatch` or `failed` read from evidence, or an `indeterminate` that holds the reservation.
- A send that names no purchase can no longer prove any purchase approved before it unsent: those are reconciled instead, and may stay `indeterminate`.
- The checkout vault is as safe as the wallet vault: against same-user malware it is obfuscation (AC-X3), and it holds a credential to read one checkout, not to pay.
- Real one-tap stays off until the store alias rule is decided: until then every real merchant whose storefront is not its myshopify domain is handed off.
- A not_sent verdict waits out the attempt window (60 s) after a crash between claiming an attempt and sending.
