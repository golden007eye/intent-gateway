# ADR-0018: Evidence pack, format v1

Status: accepted · 2026-10-17

## Context

A developer whose agent bought something may need to show a third party (a bank, a merchant, a dispute desk) what happened: what the gateway sent, that the buyer approved this exact purchase with a passkey, and what Intent Cloud reported afterwards. The local hash chain holds all of it, but it also holds every other purchase, and the third party would have to trust the developer's copy. AP2 and ACP each sketch a mandate or receipt format, but neither has a stable published format to pin, so a mapping to them waits (plan refinement 4, adapted). The pack must hold no buyer identity (invariant 3; the chain has none) and no credential, and it must claim only what was sent and what Intent Cloud signs or acknowledged (invariant 5).

## Decision

- **One file per purchase, canonical JSON.** `intent evidence <approval handle> [--out <file>]` writes `{format: "intent-gateway-evidence", v: 1, purchase, head: {seq, hash}, pinned_root: [{kid, jwk}], skeleton: [{seq, prev, hash}], entries: [...]}` as RFC 8785 JSON and a newline. A file that is not canonical, or not this shape, is refused. There is no export time and no gateway version: nothing in the pack is the exporter's word alone.
- **The skeleton is the whole chain, the entries are the purchase.** `skeleton` lists `{seq, prev, hash}` for every chain entry from genesis to the head; `entries` holds this purchase's entries whole. Each entry must hash to its own content (`sha256(JCS({seq, prev, ts, kind, body}))`) and match its skeleton row, and the skeleton must link from genesis to the head.
- **Which entries belong, decided from the entries.** One rule (`src/evidence`, `purchaseEntries`) picks them from the chain at export and checks them in the pack: the `approval_pending`, `approval` and `outcome` entries naming the handle; the cart request whose logged body is byte for byte the CartRequest chained in `sent`; the approval request an observation names; the status reads and outcome reports whose path names the handle; wallet requests whose local `purchase` names it; the search whose observation lists the offer (optional); the follow-ups and observations of those requests; and what re-checking them needs: their gateway session entries (and entry 0, which marks a demo chain), the approver pin in force at the approval, and the key statements in force. An entry outside the rule, or a pack without a pending entry and an approval, fails verify, so an entry swapped in from another purchase is caught.
- **Offline verify re-runs the chain checks.** `intent verify --evidence <file>` reads only the file: no data dir, no network, no install self-check. It checks the pack's root key against the verifier's own pin (the build's, or `--trust-keys`), then runs the checks of `intent verify` on the entries: key statements, receipts, credential and redaction rules, the approval proof (Appendix E, step 7 included), the approver pin, outcome statements and binding-v1. The numbering of a gateway session is not checked, since a pack holds only some of a session's requests. Buyer identity is scanned in what the pack says was sent (each logged body and the chained CartRequest).
- **Export only what verifies.** `intent evidence` checks the pack exactly as `intent verify --evidence` will and writes nothing when it fails. It never writes over an existing file.
- **Nothing beside the chain.** The pack is built from `audit.jsonl` alone: never the wallet vault, the wallet key, completer or session private keys. Credentials in logged requests stay `[redacted sha256:…]` markers (ADR-0014).

## Consequences

A developer can hand over one file, and the receiver can re-check every signature in it with the public contract and the root key for the host, without access to the developer's machine. The signed parts (receipts, the approval proof, key and outcome statements) are what an outsider can check without trusting the developer. The skeleton rows of entries outside the pack cannot be recomputed, so the head is the exporter's account of the chain: comparing it with a head saved earlier, or with another pack from the same chain, is what shows the chain was not rewritten. A pack shows what is in it; one built by hand could leave out an entry of the purchase (a later outcome). The pack carries other purchases only as hashes. A mapping to AP2 or ACP can be added later as another output of the same entries; format v1 stays readable.
